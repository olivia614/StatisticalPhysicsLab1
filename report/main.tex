\begin{abstract}
\noindent This report investigates the statistical mechanics of a system composed of independent binary elements (unbiased coins) as an exact computational and physical model for discrete two-state assemblies. We present a modular Python implementation that enumerates microstates, calculates binomial distribution frequencies using log-space arithmetic to prevent numerical overflow, and explores scaling behavior as $N\rightarrow10^{23}$. \section{Introduction \& Objectives}
The primary objective of this exercise is to model microstates and macroscopic properties of $N$ unbiased binary elements. The total number of microstates for $N$ coins is $2^{N}$, and the multiplicity $\Omega(N,k)$ of a macrostate with $k$ heads is given by the binomial coefficient:
\begin{equation}
\Omega(N,k) = \binom{N}{k} = \frac{N!}{k!(N-k)!}
\end{equation}
We evaluate microstate multiplicity, system entropy $S = k_{B} \ln \Omega$, and the transition to the thermodynamic limit ($N \sim 10^{23}$).

\subsection{Macrostate Frequency Distributions ($N=20, 50, 80, 100$)}
To compute $\Omega(N,k)$ for $N \in \{20,50,80,100\}$ without numerical overflow, calculations were executed in log-space using the log-gamma function:
\begin{equation}
\ln \Omega(N,k) = \ln \Gamma(N+1) - \ln \Gamma(k+1) - \ln \Gamma(N-k+1)
\end{equation}
Figure~\ref{fig:distributions} illustrates the normalized probability distributions $P_{k} = \Omega(N,k)/2^{N}$.

\subsection{Thermodynamic Limit ($N \rightarrow 10^{23}$)}
In the limit $N \rightarrow \infty$, Stirling's approximation ($\ln N! \approx N \ln N - N$) converts the binomial distribution into a Gaussian distribution centered at $\mu = N/2$ with standard deviation $\sigma = \sqrt{N}/2$. The relative fluctuation scales as:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\sigma}{\mu} = \frac{\sqrt{N}/2}{N/2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}} \xrightarrow{N \rightarrow 10^{23}} 0
\end{equation}

\subsection{Second Law of Thermodynamics Interpretation}
Spontaneous transitions toward states with lower $\Omega$ (higher order) are non-zero in probability for finite $N$, but their frequency becomes zero in practice as $N \rightarrow 10^{23}$. This demonstrates the statistical foundation of the Second Law of Thermodynamics.
